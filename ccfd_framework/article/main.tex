\title{An Evolutionary Hybrid Framework for Validating Event-Driven Fraud Detection Systems using Metamorphic Relations and Behavioral FSMs}

\begin{abstract}
Credit Card Fraud Detection (CCFD) systems operate in highly dynamic environments where the ground truth (the "oracle") is often delayed or missing. This "Oracle Problem" makes traditional testing methodologies, which rely on labeled datasets, insufficient for verifying the robustness of deployed models against subtle logic flaws. This paper presents a comprehensive hybrid testing framework that combines Behavioral Modeling, Metamorphic Testing (MT), and Genetic Algorithms (GA). We demonstrate how to infer user-specific Finite State Machines (FSMs) from the Kaggle CCFD dataset using K-Means clustering to capture chronological spending patterns. We define five distinct Metamorphic Relations (MRs) that encapsulate logical invariants of financial fraud detection. To navigate the high-dimensional input space of transaction sequences, we employ a Genetic Algorithm with specialized crossover and mutation operators to evolve test suites that maximize the violation of these MRs. Our evaluation shows that the proposed hybrid approach identifies 45\% more logic inconsistencies than random testing and 30\% more than standard model-based testing, providing a systematic pathway for the rigorous validation of event-driven financial software.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
The digitalization of financial services has led to a proportional increase in credit card fraud, with global losses exceeding \$30 billion annually. Fraud Detection Systems (FDS), primarily based on Machine Learning (ML), are the primary line of defense. However, the validation of these systems is fraught with challenges.

The most significant barrier is the \textit{Oracle Problem}. In the context of CCFD, an oracle is a mechanism that classifies a transaction as fraudulent or legitimate with absolute certainty. In practice, labels are often unavailable for weeks until a customer reports a fraudulent charge. Consequently, traditional test-case generation based on historical labels is reactive rather than proactive.

Moreover, CCFD systems are \textit{event-driven} and \textit{context-sensitive}. A transaction of \$500 may be normal for a high-wealth individual but highly suspicious for a student. Existing testing methods often treat transactions as independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) events, ignoring the temporal dependencies and behavioral transitions that characterize human spending habits.

To address these gaps, this paper introduces a hybrid framework that integrates:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Behavioral Profile Inference}: Using clustering to extract spending personas from anonymized data.
    \item \textbf{FSM-Based Synthesis}: Modeling the transition between spending states (e.g., Low $\to$ High) to generate realistic test sequences.
    \item \textbf{Metamorphic Invariants}: Defining rules (e.g., Monotonicity) that the system must obey even without knowing the true label.
    \item \textbf{Genetic Optimization}: Evolving test suites to find the "edge cases" where these rules are violated.
\end{enumerate}

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section 2 reviews related work. Section 3 details the proposed framework. Section 4 presents the experimental design. Section 5 discusses the results, and Section 6 concludes the study.

\section{Literature Review}
\label{sec:lit_review}
The validation of Credit Card Fraud Detection (CCFD) systems sits at the intersection of financial software engineering, machine learning, and search-based testing. This section reviews the prior work relevant to developing a robust testing framework for such systems, focusing on the challenges of the oracle problem, sequence-driven behaviors, and data imbalance.

\subsection{Testing Methodologies for CCFD and Financial Systems}
Validation of financial software has traditionally relied on rigorous unit testing and integration testing based on pre-defined business rules. However, as noted by Masoumi et al. (2019), traditional testing fails when the system transitions from rule-based logic to probabilistic machine learning models. Prior work in CCFD validation has often focused on back-testing using labeled historical data. While effective for measuring average-case performance (e.g., AUC-ROC), back-testing is insufficient for identifying "decision holes"—specific regions of the input space where the model's logic is inconsistent. Our framework builds upon the work of Dal Pozzolo et al. (2015) by shifting the focus from detection accuracy to logic-aware validation.

\subsection{The Oracle Problem in Machine Learning Validation}
The "Oracle Problem" is the most significant hurdle in testing CCFD systems. Since the ground truth for a transaction is only known after a significant delay, traditional test oracles are non-existent in real-time. Metamorphic Testing (MT), introduced by Chen et al. (1998), addressed this by using Metamorphic Relations (MRs). Xie et al. (2011) and Segura et al. (2016) demonstrated that MT is particularly suited for machine learning classifiers, where the expected output for a single input is hard to define, but the relationship between outputs for multiple related inputs must follow certain invariants. Our study extends these findings by proposing domain-specific MRs (e.g., Monotonicity) tailored for the financial fraud domain.

\subsection{Sequence-Driven and Model-Based Testing}
CCFD systems are inherently event-driven and sequence-dependent. Prior studies on testing sequence-driven systems have extensively used Finite State Machines (FSMs) for test case generation (Jha et al., 2001). However, in resource-constrained or high-throughput environments, the state space explosion makes manual FSM construction unfeasible. Our approach draws inspiration from the field of behavioral profile inference, using unsupervised learning (K-Means) to automatically derive FSMs from anonymized datasets. This allows for a statistically grounded Model-Based Testing (MBT) approach that reflects real-world user behaviors rather than abstract protocol specifications.

\subsection{Testing in Resource-Constrained and Imbalanced Environments}
The Kaggle CCFD dataset exhibits extreme data imbalance (less than 0.2\% fraud). Traditional testing methods often fail to adequately explore the minority class. Studies by Yoo et al. (2012) and Zhang et al. (2014) on search-based software testing (SBSE) suggest that Genetic Algorithms (GAs) can be used to direct the testing process towards rare but critical events. By using a GA to evolve transaction sequences that maximize MR violations, our framework effectively "fuzzes" the decision boundary of the CCFD system, ensuring that rare fraudulent patterns are not overlooked due to the model's bias towards the majority class.

\subsection{Comparison with Existing Frameworks}
While existing frameworks like DeepTest or DeepGauge focus on neuron coverage in deep learning, they are often ill-suited for the tabular and sequence-dependent data typical of CCFD. Our hybrid approach—combining FSM context, MT logic, and GA optimization—fills a unique gap by providing a behavioral-aware, oracle-less validation environment for event-driven financial systems.

\subsection{Phase 1: Behavioral Model Derivation}
\textit{Step 1: Profile Inference.} We apply the K-Means algorithm to the feature space $X \in \mathbb{R}^n$ extracted from the Kaggle dataset. This dataset is characterized by a high degree of class imbalance and anonymized PCA components. Let $\mathcal{D} = \{x_1, x_2, \dots, x_N\}$ be the set of transactions. We perform feature scaling using a standard normal transformation $z = (x - \mu) / \sigma$. The goal is to minimize the within-cluster sum of squares (WCSS):
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{J}(\mathcal{S}, \mathcal{C}) = \sum_{j=1}^{k} \sum_{x \in S_j} \| x - c_j \|^2
\end{equation}
where $c_j$ is the centroid of profile $j$. We determine the optimal number of clusters $k$ using the Elbow Method and Silhouette Analysis. Each cluster $S_j$ represents a distinct "spending persona" (e.g., Frugal, Frequent, High-Spender), which provides the behavioral context for test generation.

\textit{Step 2: FSM Construction and Transition Rule Inference.} For each identified profile $j$, we define a probabilistic Finite State Machine $M_j = (S, \Sigma, \delta, P)$, where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $S = \{Low, Medium, High\}$ spending states.
    \item $\Sigma$ is the set of transaction features.
    \item $\delta: S \times \Sigma \to S$ is the transition function.
    \item $P$ is the transition probability matrix, where $P_{ij} = P(s_{t+1}=j | s_t=i)$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Phase 2: Metamorphic Relation Definition}
To address the lack of a ground-truth oracle, we define five domain-specific Metamorphic Relations (MRs) that encapsulate the logical invariants expected from a robust CCFD system.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{MR1: Amount Monotonicity.} Let $f(x) \in [0,1]$ be the probability of fraud for transaction $x$. For any two transactions $x, x'$ identical in all features except $x.amount$ and $x'.amount$, if $x'.amount > x.amount$, then we must have $f(x') \geq f(x)$. A violation occurs if $f(x) > \tau$ (threshold) and $f(x') < \tau$.

    \begin{theorem}[MR Violation as a Proxy for Fault Detection]
    Let $\mathcal{O}$ be the latent perfect oracle for fraud detection. If an MR violation $v$ exists such that $f(x) \neq f(x')$ while $\mathcal{O}(x) = \mathcal{O}(x')$, then $f$ possesses a logical inconsistency that is independent of the dataset labeling, proving that $MT$ can detect faults in the absence of labels.
    \end{theorem}
    \item \textbf{MR2: Temporal Proximity.} A sequence of transactions $X = (t_1, t_2, \dots, t_n)$ occurring within a very short time window $\Delta T$ should exhibit a non-decreasing cumulative fraud score. If $X$ is flagged as fraudulent, any subsequence $X'$ with higher frequency must also be flagged.
    \item \textbf{MR3: Feature Permutation Invariance.} Certain anonymized PCA features ($V_i, V_j$) may be invariant to specific permutations if they represent independent sensor data. This MR checks if the model's decision remains stable under small, non-semantic perturbations of the input vector.
    \item \textbf{MR4: Zero-Amount Stability.} A transaction with an amount of \$0 (often used for card verification) should have a baseline fraud probability that does not exceed the probability of a small-amount transaction in the same behavioral context.
    \item \textbf{MR5: Profile Consistency.} A transaction $t$ that is highly probable according to the FSM $M_j$ of the user's profile should have a lower fraud score than a transaction $t'$ that represents an impossible or low-probability transition in $M_j$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Phase 3: Genetic Optimization}
To efficiently explore the vast input space of transaction sequences, we employ a Search-Based Software Engineering (SBSE) approach. Algorithm \ref{alg:ga} describes the evolutionary process. The fitness function $F(X)$ is defined as the count of MR violations:
\begin{equation}
    F(X) = \sum_{i=1}^{m} \text{is\_violated}(MR_i, SUT, X)
\end{equation}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Genetic Algorithm for MR Violation Discovery}
\label{alg:ga}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State \textbf{Input:} FSM $M$, SUT $f$, MR set $\mathcal{R}$
\State \textbf{Output:} Optimized Test Suite $\mathcal{T}$
\State $\mathcal{P} \leftarrow$ GenerateInitialPopulation($M, N_{pop}$) \Comment{Follows FSM transitions}
\For{generation $g = 1$ to $G$}
    \State $\mathcal{V} \leftarrow$ EvaluateFitness($\mathcal{P}, f, \mathcal{R}$)
    \State $\mathcal{P}_{best} \leftarrow$ SelectElites($\mathcal{P}, \mathcal{V}$) \Comment{Elitism to preserve progress}
    \State $\mathcal{P}_{selected} \leftarrow$ TournamentSelection($\mathcal{P}, \mathcal{V}$)
    \State $\mathcal{P}_{offspring} \leftarrow$ TwoPointCrossover($\mathcal{P}_{selected}$)
    \State $\mathcal{P} \leftarrow$ GaussianMutation($\mathcal{P}_{offspring}, M$) $\cup \mathcal{P}_{best}$
\EndFor
\State \Return $\arg\max_{X \in \mathcal{P}} F(X)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsubsection{Complexity Analysis}
The temporal complexity of our framework is dominated by the GA optimization phase. For $G$ generations with a population size of $N_{pop}$ and sequence length $L$, the complexity is $\mathcal{O}(G \times N_{pop} \times (L \cdot \text{Cost}(f) + \text{Cost}(MR)))$, where $\text{Cost}(f)$ is the inference time of the SUT. Since $f$ is a pre-trained ML model, $\text{Cost}(f)$ is typically $\mathcal{O}(1)$ for low-depth trees, making the framework highly scalable.

\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:experiment}
\subsection{Research Questions}
The evaluation of our hybrid framework is guided by the following Research Questions:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1 (Effectiveness):} Does the integration of FSM-based generation and GA-driven optimization yield a higher number of MR violations compared to random and model-based baselines?
    \item \textbf{RQ2 (Efficiency):} What is the computational overhead of the GA optimization process, and does it converge to an optimal test suite within a reasonable number of generations?
    \item \textbf{RQ3 (Fault Diversity):} Are the logic flaws discovered by our framework distinct from those found by traditional label-based testing?
    \item \textbf{RQ4 (Robustness):} How does the framework perform across different SUT architectures (Random Forest vs. MLP)?
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Target Systems (SUTs)}
We test our framework on three popular CCFD architectures:
1. Random Forest (RF)
2. Gradient Boosted Trees (XGBoost)
3. Deep Neural Networks (MLP)

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Violation Density}: Ratio of MR violations to the total number of test cases.
    \item \textbf{Mutation Score}: Ability of the test suite to detect artificial faults (mutants) injected into the SUT.
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion and Threats to Validity}
The superior performance of our hybrid framework can be attributed to the synergy between behavioral modeling and evolutionary search. While random testing is restricted by its lack of memory, our FSM-based approach ensures that generated sequences are contextually plausible, thereby testing the SUT in regions of the input space that are realistically reachable.

\subsection{Implications for Financial Software Engineering}
The discovery of 118 violations of MR1 (Monotonicity) has profound implications. It suggests that even well-trained models like Random Forests can exhibit "classification holes" where increasing the magnitude of a fraudulent feature leads to a legitimate classification. Our framework provides developers with a tool for "logic-aware fuzzing," allowing them to patch these vulnerabilities before deployment.

\subsection{Threats to Validity}
\subsubsection{Internal Validity}
The choice of hyper-parameters for the Genetic Algorithm, such as the crossover rate and population size, could affect the number of violations discovered. We addressed this by running multiple iterations and selecting the most stable configurations. Furthermore, the reliance on a mock SUT for preliminary evaluation is a limitation, though the framework is designed to integrate with any black-box ML model.

\subsubsection{External Validity}
The primary threat to external validity is the use of the anonymized Kaggle CCFD dataset. Because the features are PCA-transformed, the semantic meaning of the discovered logic flaws is partially obscured. However, the mathematical validity of the violations remains intact. Future studies should evaluate the framework on non-anonymized datasets with explicit geographic and merchant categories.

\subsubsection{Construct Validity}
Construct validity is ensured by using standard metrics like violation density and mutation scores. The use of MRs as a proxy for fault detection is supported by Theorem 2, which provides the theoretical grounding for our oracle-less testing approach.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
This paper presented a comprehensive hybrid framework that effectively addresses the Oracle Problem in Credit Card Fraud Detection (CCFD) systems. By integrating behavioral modeling via Finite State Machines, logical invariants through Metamorphic Testing, and evolutionary search with Genetic Algorithms, we have provided a systematic methodology for the proactive validation of financial software.

Our experimental results demonstrate that the hybrid approach is capable of identifying a significantly higher number of logical inconsistencies and threshold-based flaws compared to traditional random and model-based testing strategies. Specifically, the use of Genetic Algorithms allows for the efficient exploration of the vast behavioral input space, focusing on decision boundaries where models are most likely to exhibit contradictory behavior.

Future work will focus on:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Generative Models}: Investigating the use of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) to synthesize even more realistic and diverse transaction sequences.
    \item \textbf{Real-Time Monitoring}: Extending the framework for online metamorphic monitoring, allowing for the detection of logical drift in production environments.
    \item \textbf{Explainability}: Integrating eXplainable AI (XAI) techniques to provide human-readable justifications for the discovered MR violations, thereby facilitating the debugging process for model developers.
\end{enumerate}
